Presentation Design

What is Presentation Design?

Presentation design is the process of creating visual slides that communicate ideas effectively to an audience. It involves combining text, images, layout, and storytelling to deliver a compelling message — whether in a classroom, boardroom, or conference.

🎯 Key Principles of Effective Presentations

1. Simplicity

One idea per slide. Avoid clutter and excessive text.

2. Visual Hierarchy

Guide the audience's eye using size, color, and placement.

3. Consistency

Use uniform fonts, colors, and layouts throughout.

4. Contrast & Readability

Dark text on light backgrounds (or vice versa). Minimum 24pt font.

5. The 10-20-30 Rule

10 slides, 20 minutes, 30pt minimum font (Guy Kawasaki).

6. Purposeful Visuals

Every image, chart, or icon should support your message.

📌 Real-Life Scenario 1

Situation: Maria, a marketing intern, is tasked to present a quarterly social media report to her team lead. She has 15 minutes.

What she does: She creates 8 slides using a company-branded template. Each slide has one key metric (e.g., "Instagram engagement up 23%") with a simple bar chart. She uses bullet points only for the action items slide. The final slide is a clear "Next Steps" summary.

Why it works: Minimal text, visual data, consistent branding, and a clear call-to-action. Her manager immediately understands the results without reading paragraphs.

📌 Real-Life Scenario 2

Situation: A group of IT students must pitch their capstone project (a mobile health app) to a panel of faculty judges in 10 minutes.

What they do: They use a dark-themed slide deck with the app's color palette. Slide 1 is a hook ("1 in 3 Filipinos skip doctor visits due to cost"). They include app mockup screenshots, a system architecture diagram, and end with a live demo link. No slide has more than 6 lines of text.

Why it works: The audience is engaged by the hook, the visuals show rather than tell, and the technical diagram demonstrates credibility without overwhelming the judges.

Common Presentation Mistakes to Avoid

  • ✗ Reading directly from slides (slides are visual aids, not scripts)
  • ✗ Using too many fonts or colors (stick to 2 fonts, 3–4 colors max)
  • ✗ Overloading slides with paragraphs of text
  • ✗ Using low-quality or irrelevant images
  • ✗ Ignoring slide transitions (too many flashy effects distract)
  • ✗ No clear structure (introduction → body → conclusion)

Technical Writing

What is Technical Writing?

Technical writing is the practice of creating clear, concise, and well-structured documents that communicate complex or specialized information to a specific audience. It prioritizes accuracy, clarity, and usability over creative expression.

Examples include: user manuals, SOPs, project proposals, system documentation, reports, emails, and help guides.

🎯 Core Principles of Technical Writing

1. Clarity

Use simple, direct language. Avoid jargon unless your audience understands it. "Click the Save button" is better than "Actuate the persistence mechanism."

2. Conciseness

Remove unnecessary words. Every sentence should serve a purpose. "Due to the fact that" → "Because."

3. Structure

Use headings, numbered lists, bullet points, and tables to organize information logically. Readers scan before they read.

4. Audience Awareness

Write for your reader's knowledge level. A user guide for seniors differs greatly from developer API documentation.

5. Accuracy

Every fact, instruction, and reference must be verifiable and correct. Errors in technical documents cause real-world failures.

6. Consistency

Use the same terms, formatting, and tone throughout. If you call it "Dashboard" on page 1, don't call it "Control Panel" on page 5.

📌 Real-Life Scenario 3

Situation: Jake, a junior developer at a startup, is asked to write a README file for a new internal tool so other team members can set it up.

What he writes:

# Inventory Sync Tool

Syncs product inventory between Shopify and our warehouse DB every 15 minutes.

## Requirements

- Node.js 18+

- .env file with API keys (see .env.example)

## Setup

1. Clone the repo

2. Run `npm install`

3. Copy `.env.example` to `.env` and fill in credentials

4. Run `npm start`

Why it works: Clear heading structure, numbered steps for sequence, prerequisites listed upfront, and no unnecessary prose. A new teammate can get the tool running in minutes.

📌 Real-Life Scenario 4

Situation: An office admin needs to write an SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) for how staff should request IT equipment.

What she writes:

SOP: IT Equipment Request

Purpose: To standardize equipment requests and ensure timely processing.

Scope: All full-time employees.

Procedure:

1. Fill out Form IT-REQ-01 (available on the intranet under Forms > IT).

2. Get your department head's signature.

3. Submit the signed form to the IT Help Desk (Room 204) or email it to ithelpdesk@company.com.

4. Allow 5–7 business days for processing.

Notes: Requests over ₱50,000 require additional approval from Finance.

Why it works: Purpose and scope defined immediately, numbered steps for the process, and edge cases addressed in notes. No ambiguity about what to do.

Technical Writing vs. Other Writing

Aspect Technical Writing Creative Writing
Purpose Inform, instruct Entertain, inspire
Tone Objective, neutral Subjective, expressive
Structure Rigid, formatted Flexible, free-form
Audience Specific, defined General public
Language Plain, precise Figurative, varied

📄 Common Technical Documents in the Workplace

User Manual
Step-by-step guide for end users (e.g., how to use a printer)
SOP
Standardized procedure for repeatable tasks
Project Proposal
Document to pitch a project with objectives, timeline, budget
Incident Report
Record of an event/issue with facts, timeline, and resolution
Meeting Minutes
Summary of discussion points, decisions, and action items
Email (Formal)
Professional correspondence with clear subject and purpose

Individual Activity

📝 Activity 1: "Fix This Slide"

Objective: Apply presentation design principles by identifying and correcting common slide mistakes.

The "Bad" Slide (Imagine this on a projected screen):

WHY OUR COMPANY NEEDS A NEW WEBSITE (and also some other things we want to talk about today including budget)

Our current website was built in 2018 and it uses outdated technology. The bounce rate is 67% which is very high compared to industry average of 40%. We conducted a survey of 200 customers and 78% said the site was "hard to navigate." The mobile experience is particularly bad because the site is not responsive. We believe a redesign would cost approximately ₱500,000-₱800,000 depending on the agency. The timeline would be 3-6 months. We also want to integrate a chatbot and update our product catalog and maybe add a blog section. Our competitors like ABC Corp and XYZ Inc both launched new sites last year. We recommend moving forward with a proposal from Digital Agency Co.

*** PLEASE DO NOT SHARE THIS SLIDE OUTSIDE THE TEAM ***

Instructions:

  1. List at least 5 problems with the slide above.
  2. Redesign it into 2–3 properly designed slides (sketch on paper or use any presentation tool).
  3. Write a brief explanation (3–5 sentences) of what you changed and why.

Submission: Upload your redesigned slides + explanation as a PDF.

📝 Activity 2: "Write a Mini SOP"

Objective: Apply technical writing principles to create a clear, structured procedure document.

Instructions:

Choose ONE of the following everyday tasks and write a short SOP (Standard Operating Procedure) for it:

  • How to connect to the school Wi-Fi
  • How to submit an assignment on your LMS (e.g., Google Classroom, Canvas)
  • How to report a lost school ID to the registrar

Your SOP must include:

  • Title
  • Purpose (1–2 sentences)
  • Scope (who this is for)
  • Step-by-step procedure (numbered, 5–8 steps)
  • At least one "Note" or "Important" callout

Submission: Submit as a Word document or PDF. Proper formatting required.

Group Activity

👥 "Pitch It Right" — Group Presentation + Documentation

Group Size: 3–5 members

Scenario:

Your group works at a small company. Management has asked you to propose a new internal tool or system improvement. You must deliver both a presentation and a written proposal document.

Choose a topic (or create your own):

  • A new employee attendance tracking app
  • Switching from email to a team messaging platform (e.g., Slack)
  • Implementing a digital filing system for HR documents
  • Creating an FAQ chatbot for the company website

Deliverables:

A. Slide Deck (7–10 slides)

  • Title slide with group members
  • Problem statement
  • Proposed solution
  • Key features/benefits
  • Implementation timeline
  • Estimated cost (can be fictional)
  • Conclusion/recommendation

B. Written Proposal (1–2 pages)

  • Formal heading with date and recipient
  • Executive summary (2–3 sentences)
  • Problem description
  • Proposed solution with details
  • Budget table
  • Timeline
  • Prepared by (group members)

Presentation:

Each group will present for 8–10 minutes. All members must speak. Q&A follows (2 minutes).

Grading Criteria:

  • • Slide design & visual quality (25%)
  • • Written proposal clarity & structure (25%)
  • • Content & feasibility of the proposal (25%)
  • • Presentation delivery & teamwork (25%)
